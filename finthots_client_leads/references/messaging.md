# Messaging: sequence, openers, and the real proof points

## The sequence, and why it is in this order

**Touch 1 — L&D or training head.** They hold the eLearning library budget and the annual
training calendar. Lead with production quality and a credential, not with pain they do not
own.

**Touch 2 — credit, risk or business head.** They do not hold the budget, but they feel the
pain and they create the demand that forces L&D to spend. This is often the faster-converting
entry point for functional content. Open by saying explicitly why you are writing to them
rather than to HR.

**Touch 3 — compliance.** Mandatory training is annual, regulator-driven and already
budgeted. The question is not whether it is needed but who delivers it this year. Shortest
sales cycle on the list, lowest ticket, recurring.

**Then HR** (sponsor at smaller lenders, owns induction and mass hire-and-train), **then the
verified nodal officer** (referral play, not a pitch), **then the executive.**

At Tier 3 the executive moves up to touch 1 or 2, because at that size the MD or CBO signs
off a ₹10–15 lakh spend directly. At Tier 1 and 2, never lead with the CEO — no reply, and it
signals you do not know the industry.

## What to open with, by persona

**L&D head.** "I am writing to you rather than to your business heads, because the eLearning
library decision sits with L&D." Then the library: 200+ hours of BFSI functional content,
SCORM-compliant, ready for their LMS, nine Indian languages. Then a proof point. Then a
fifteen-minute ask with an explicit out.

**Credit or business head.** "I am writing to you rather than to HR. In my experience credit
heads buy functional training and L&D buys libraries, and it is the first I want to talk
about." Then the ex-banker credibility. Then the specific pattern: sourcing sets an
expectation credit was never going to approve, and property visits get done without a
framework — both surface in the portfolio eighteen months later, and both are training
problems rather than process ones. Then offer to take it to L&D with their read on the problem.

**Compliance officer.** A direct question, not a pitch: "for FY27, are you delivering
mandatory training in-house or through an outside provider?" Then why in-house costs more than
it looks — someone builds the deck, chases completions, maintains the evidence trail for the
NHB inspection, redoes it when the circular moves. Then the audit trail and completion
dashboard.

**HR head.** Field capability building, induction, mass hire-and-train. The Middle East bank
programme is the strongest proof here.

**Executive at a small lender.** "At your size the training decision is a business one, not an
HR formality." Then one question rather than a pitch: when you add twenty field officers this
year, what do their first ninety days look like, and is any of it structured?

**Nodal or grievance officer.** Explicitly not a pitch. "The training budget is not yours. You
see every complaint, which makes you the one person who knows which process is actually
failing." Ask for ten minutes of their read, and for the name of whoever owns training.

**Existing or past client.** Open as a follow-up, never cold. Two things: whether the earlier
modules are still in use and still current, and the rest of the library. Close with an
invitation to say plainly if the earlier work did not land well internally — it is disarming
and it surfaces real objections.

## Pricing, for the proposal stage only

eLearning is priced per hour of finished content by design level and volume tier (L1 through
L3, Explorer through Master). Do not quote in a first email. The wallet offer document holds
the grid.

## Writing rules

- One company-specific sentence in every message. Not a merge field — a real observation
  about their book: informal income, rural vernacular, a rebrand, a bank parent, a group L&D
  structure.
- Short. Four paragraphs plus a signature.
- Always give an out: "if the budget sits with L&D, say so and I will take it there."
- Never write in the third person about Manish in a message Manish signs.
- Plain subject lines. Nothing that reads like a campaign.
