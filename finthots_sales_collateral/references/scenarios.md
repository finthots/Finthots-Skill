# Pain-point scenarios — angle 14

The strongest cold opener FinThots has. Instead of opening with who FinThots is, open with
something that happens in the reader's own business, say where it costs them, and name the
module already built for it.

**Every scenario below maps to content FinThots has actually built.** That is what keeps the
angle safe. A scenario that is slightly wrong proves the sender has never done the job, which
is the exact opposite of what the angle is for. Do not improvise new ones in a live
conversation.

## The four beats, in this order

1. **The scene.** One concrete thing a named role does on a specific day. Not "gaps in
   underwriting capability" — an officer parking outside a site and photographing the gate.
2. **The cost.** Where it surfaces and when. Mortgage pain is almost always delayed, so say
   the delay out loud: *"it surfaces at the first missed EMI"*, *"it shows up in your book
   eighteen months later"*. That delay is the reason nobody has fixed it yet.
3. **What changes.** Name the actual module or workshop, not a capability. "A simulated site
   the officer is scored on" beats "experiential learning solutions".
4. **The ask.** Small. A 30-minute capability review, or the free skill assessment. Never a
   demo of the whole library.

## The rules

- **One scenario per message.** Two reads as a brochure and the reader picks neither.
- **Only scenarios with content behind them.** A scenario FinThots cannot solve wins the
  meeting and loses it in the room.
- **Never name a company or a person** — not the prospect, not another lender, not a client.
  "An NBFC mortgage team" is enough and is safe.
- **Never invent the cost figure.** Describe where the loss appears; do not quantify it. A
  credit head knows his own numbers and will catch an invented one instantly.
- **Use their vocabulary** — tranche, LTV, search report, login-to-sanction, FOIR, TAT — but
  only the terms you are sure of. One wrong term undoes the whole message.
- **If they argue with the scene, that is the win.** An argument about whether it happens that
  way is already a conversation about their process.

## The twelve scenarios

### Credit and business

**1. Property visit done from the car.** The officer parks outside, photographs the gate and
the nameplate, files the report, never walks the floor — so nobody records that the first
floor slab has not been cast. *Cost:* the next tranche is released against construction that
does not exist, and it surfaces at the first missed EMI. *Fix:* the gamified Property Visit
module built for an NBFC mortgage team, where the officer walks a simulated site and is scored
on what he failed to check. *To:* Head of Credit, CCO, Head of Technical & Legal.
*Cold line:* "A property visit filed from the car looks identical on paper to one done
properly. The difference only shows up at the tranche release."

**2. Informal income assessed on gut feel.** A self-employed borrower, no ITR, a handwritten
sales note. Two officers in the same branch land ₹20,000 apart on monthly income. *Cost:*
either good customers are declined or the book absorbs files it should not have — and neither
shows in a dashboard, because both look like normal underwriting. *Fix:* informal income
assessment within the affordable-housing credit workshops. *To:* National Credit Manager, Head
of Credit Policy, Head of Affordable Housing.
*Cold line:* "If two of your officers assess the same informal-income file twenty thousand
apart, the policy is not the problem — the method was never taught."

**3. Sourcing promises what credit will never approve.** The DSA tells the customer it will be
sanctioned; credit declines on an LTV the DSA never understood; the customer walks and sales
files it under "credit is too tight". *Cost:* login-to-sanction falls, TAT complaints reach
the nodal officer, and the two functions stop talking — which is the expensive part. *Fix:*
the 4-hour Credit Policy for Mortgage Loan business, taught to the sourcing side rather than
only to credit. *To:* CBO, National Sales Manager, Head of Credit.
*Cold line:* "Sourcing sets an expectation credit was never going to approve. Both sides think
the other is the problem, and the customer is the one who leaves."

**4. Title cleared on a photocopy.** A vernacular document nobody reads properly; a
one-generation gap in the chain that the search report notes in a line everyone skims.
*Cost:* the mortgage is found unenforceable at the one moment it matters — recovery. *Fix:*
technical and legal content within the mortgage credit curriculum. *To:* Head of Technical &
Legal, Head of Credit, Head of Legal.
*Cold line:* "A gap in the title chain costs nothing until recovery, and everything after it."

### Collections and conduct

**6. The collections call that becomes a complaint.** A field officer raises his voice at a
delinquent borrower's door in front of the neighbours. He recovers nothing and creates a
witness. *Cost:* a fair-practices complaint that reaches the nodal officer and then the
regulator, worth many times the recovery. *Fix:* collections simulations — teach me / try me /
test me, in 9 languages. *To:* Head of Collections, CCO, nodal officer (as a question only).
*Cold line:* "One raised voice at a borrower's door creates a witness and recovers nothing.
The conversation is trainable; the complaint is not retractable."

### Compliance

**7. KYC done to fill the form, not to catch anything.** Every document collected and ticked;
nobody notices three files in one branch sharing an address and a mobile number. *Cost:* a
small fraud ring that surfaces at the first EMI bounce, and an audit finding that the controls
existed and were followed. *Fix:* role-based KYC-AML, Risk and Corporate Governance (6 hours)
and Fraud Risk Management (5 hours) — pattern recognition, not document checklists. *To:* CCO,
Principal Officer AML/KYC, Head of Fraud Risk.
*Cold line:* "KYC that is completed correctly and catches nothing is the most expensive kind
of compliance, because it passes audit."

**12. POSH signed off, nobody knows the route.** The annual session delivered as a slide-read
and signed off; ask three employees how a complaint is raised and get three answers. *Cost:*
an incident handled wrongly in the first week, by people acting in good faith. *Fix:* POSH
sensitivity training delivered for an entire NBFC — eLearning followed by a live intervention.
*To:* CCO, CHRO, Presiding Officer of the Internal Committee.
*Cold line:* "A signed-off POSH session where nobody can describe the complaint route is a
compliance record, not a protection."

### L&D

**8. The policy circular nobody read.** A policy change goes out as a 40-page PDF attached to
an email; four weeks later branches are still underwriting to the old LTV and believe they are
compliant. *Cost:* a policy breach found in audit, dated to the four weeks everyone thought
the circular had been "communicated". *Fix:* policy decoding into short mobile modules with a
knowledge check. *To:* Head of Credit Policy, Head of L&D, Head of Internal Audit.
*Cold line:* "A 40-page circular emailed to the field is a record that you sent it, not
evidence that anyone can apply it."

**9. Training happened and nothing changed.** A classroom day, a feedback form rating it 4.6,
and the same error rate next quarter. *Cost:* at year end L&D cannot defend the budget with
anything but attendance and a smile sheet, so next year it is cut. *Fix:* skill assessment
before and after plus a gap report — the model used on the Middle East retail assets programme
and the Training Impact Assessment for a public sector bank. *To:* Head of L&D, CLO, CHRO.
*Cold line:* "A 4.6 feedback score tells you the room was comfortable. It does not tell you
what changed, and that is the number you will be asked for."

**10. The officer cannot read the module.** Content in English; the officer works in Marathi,
or Telugu, or Oriya. He opens it once, understands a third, and does not return. *Cost:*
completion is reported and capability is unchanged — the worst combination, because the
dashboard says the problem is solved. *Fix:* 32 hours of functional eLearning across 6
businesses in 9 languages, produced in-house. *To:* Head of L&D, Head of Digital Learning,
business head of a field-heavy vertical.
*Cold line:* "A module in English and an officer who works in Telugu produce a completion
report and no change in capability."

**11. The branch that cannot be pulled off the floor.** Forty officers, month-end every month,
and a two-day classroom that would empty the branch. Deferred, then deferred again. *Cost:*
four quarters pass, the gap is four quarters wider, and the budget lapsed unspent. *Fix:*
mobile micro-modules taken between two property visits, with short vILT only where a trainer
is genuinely needed. *To:* Head of L&D, Regional Business Head, Head of Retail Business.
*Cold line:* "Training that needs the branch emptied gets deferred until the budget lapses.
Nobody decided against it; it just never had a free week."

### HR and induction

**5. The new joiner is on the field in week one.** No induction slot for six weeks, so he
learns the product from whoever sits beside him — and inherits that person's shortcuts along
with their knowledge. *Cost:* time-to-productivity stretches, and the mis-sell complaint
arrives in month three with the customer quoting something he was told on day four. *Fix:*
functional induction eLearning, built for 12 retail businesses of an NBFC and for 9 businesses
in 2 languages. *To:* CHRO, Head of Talent Acquisition, Head of HR Operations.
*Cold line:* "A field officer who learns the product from the person beside him inherits their
shortcuts too. That is what surfaces in month three."

## Why it works, and where it fails

Every other training vendor in that inbox opens with who they are. A scenario opens with the
reader's own Tuesday. It proves the sender has done the job before it asks for anything, which
is the only credential a credit head cares about.

It fails when the scene is slightly wrong, because that proves the opposite just as fast. The
twelve above are drawn from modules FinThots has built for lenders, which is what keeps them
safe to send.
