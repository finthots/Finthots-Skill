# The fourteen pitch angles

Seven came from Manish; seven were added. Each carries a readiness status. **Do not use an
angle marked NOT YET** — the proof does not exist, and the claim will be challenged.

**Angle 14, the pain-point scenario, is the default cold opener** where there is no referral
and no prior relationship. Its twelve ready-made scenarios are in `references/scenarios.md`.

---

## 1. Outcome numbers — NOT YET

*"Conversion went from X% to Y% after the programme."*

**Buyer:** credit / business head, executive.
**Proof held:** impact assessment is a real capability — the Middle East bank programme
included learner impact assessment, and FinThots ran a Training Impact Assessment for a
public sector bank.
**Proof missing:** the actual figures. No documented before/after percentages exist.

**Why it is blocked.** A credit head will ask how it was measured, over what period, against
what control. An invented number ends the deal and the relationship. Angle 4 is this same
promise in the future tense and is honest — use that instead, deliver it, and this angle
becomes available for the *next* prospect. The two are one angle at different times.

## 2. Cost — READY, but never first

*"Roughly 20% below an in-house build or another agency, with L2/L3 production included."*

**Buyer:** L&D, HR, executive.
**Proof held:** the wallet pricing grid across L1–L3 design levels and volume tiers; in-house
studio and ID team, so no subcontracting margin.

**Caution.** Never lead on price with the budget holder — it anchors FinThots as disposable,
and buyers who arrive on price leave on price. Hold for the proposal, or for when they raise
budget first. The stronger version is not a discount claim but their own arithmetic:
cost per learner per year against what an in-house build costs them in salary, calendar and
rework.

## 3. Fits existing habits — READY and underused

*"Mobile-first, short modules, no classroom time — a field officer learns between visits
without changing how they work."*

**Buyer:** L&D, HR, credit/business at field-heavy lenders.
**Proof held:** SCORM on their own LMS, nine Indian languages, L1–L3 levels, in-house studio.

For affordable housing, rural and micro-housing lenders — officers in the field all day who
cannot sit in a classroom — this is the entire argument. Say it concretely: *"between two
property visits, on his own phone, in Marathi."* Abstractions like "flexible learning" waste
the angle.

## 4. We measure the improvement — READY, and the one to lead with

*"Not a course library — a programme with assessment before and after, so you can see what
changed."*

**Buyer:** L&D, executive.
**Proof held:** impact assessment in the Middle East bank programme; Training Impact
Assessment delivered for a public sector bank; skill assessment and role-wise learning
journey mapping are existing listed offerings.

This answers the L&D head's real fear: having to justify the spend at year end. It is also
the engine that generates Angle 1 for future pitches.

## 5. Case studies and testimonials — NOT YET, must be built

**Proof held:** strong raw material — RBI Ethics module, Property Visit gamified module,
Middle East retail assets programme, 32 hours of vehicle finance eLearning in nine languages,
SME credit for a private bank.
**Proof missing:** the artefacts themselves. These are currently one-line project
descriptions in a profile deck, not case studies.

Highest-value thing to build; costs nothing but time. The Middle East programme is the best
candidate — it was made mandatory for every new joiner, which is the strongest endorsement
available.

## 6. Workshops for time-poor and existing clients — READY

*"Short, sharp instructor-led sessions instead of a library — high impact, low calendar
cost."*

**Buyer:** credit/business heads, existing clients.
**Proof held:** RBI College of Supervisors workshops; credit underwriting workshops across
unsecured, MSME, CV-CE, used car, mortgage and affordable housing; 16,000+ trained personally.

A credit head will not buy a library but will give you a day. The workshop is also the
cheapest route to the people who later create demand for the library.

## 7. Pilot before commitment — READY once priced

*"One module, built and impact-assessed, before any library commitment."*

Removes the single biggest cold objection — *we don't know if it will work for our people* —
and manufactures the Angle 1 data. Risk reversal beats persuasion on a first contact.

## 8. Regulatory obligation, not preference — READY

*"The mandatory set is not a want. The only question is who delivers FY27."*

**Buyer:** compliance only.
**Proof held:** KYC-AML, compliance, risk and governance, fraud risk and POSH already built;
the Ethics and Etiquettes module was built **for the Reserve Bank of India**.

A completely different buying motive from every other angle: obligation, not improvement.
Budget is annual and already approved. Shortest sales cycle available. The RBI credential
does the entire job — a regulator has accepted the production standard.

## 9. Speed to deploy — READY

*"200+ hours already built. Weeks to deploy, not the six months an in-house build takes."*

**Buyer:** L&D, executive, anyone scaling fast.

Distinct from cost and often more persuasive. A lender adding 200 field officers this year
cannot wait six months. Pairs well with Angle 3 — fast to deploy *and* no disruption.

## 10. Nine Indian languages — READY, and a moat

*"Content in the language the field officer actually works in, produced in-house."*

**Proof held:** 32 hours of vehicle finance eLearning delivered in nine languages; collections
simulations in nine languages; in-house recording studio; localisation as a listed service.

Most competitors cannot do this at all, or subcontract it badly. For rural, affordable,
micro-housing and MFI lenders it is close to decisive. Name the specific languages their
field map needs — never the word "multilingual".

## 11. Credibility by association — READY

*"Built for the Reserve Bank of India. 40+ BFSI clients. Founders who ran credit at State
Bank, PNB and Axis."*

Not an angle on its own — it is what makes any other angle get read. One line, early, in
every first message.

## 12. Attrition economics — READY once the model exists

*"Field attrition has a cost per head. Structured induction cuts time-to-productivity, so the
cost per leaver falls."*

**Buyer:** HR, talent acquisition, executive.
**Proof held:** induction eLearning for 12 retail businesses of an NBFC; mass hire-and-train
project experience.

The only angle framed in money rather than capability, which makes it the one a CFO or a
Tier 3 MD understands immediately.

## 13. We already know your problem — READY, best cold opener for credit

*"Sourcing sets an expectation credit was never going to approve, and property visits get
done without a framework. Both surface in the portfolio eighteen months later."*

**Buyer:** credit / business head.
**Proof held:** the gamified Property Visit module; the 4-hour mortgage Credit Policy course;
eleven years in lending.

Diagnostic credibility beats any claim about yourself. Saying their problem back to them
accurately, before asking for anything, separates FinThots from every other training vendor
in their inbox.

## 14. Pain-point scenario — tell them their own Tuesday — READY, and the best cold opener here

*"Here is a specific thing that happens in your business, here is where it costs you, and here
is the module we already built for it."*

**Buyer:** all of them, with a different scenario each.
**Proof held:** twelve scenarios in `references/scenarios.md`, every one mapped to content
FinThots has already built — property visit, informal income, mortgage credit policy,
collections conduct, KYC-AML, fraud risk, induction, POSH, nine languages.
**Proof missing:** nothing. This is sendable today.

Angle 13 is a one-line diagnostic; this is the full four-beat story with the fix attached:
scene → cost → what changes → a small ask. It works because it inverts the usual order — the
reader's problem first, FinThots second, the ask last.

**The discipline that keeps it safe:** use only the twelve scenarios, never improvise a new one
in a live conversation, and never put a number on what it costs them. A scene that is slightly
wrong proves the sender has never done the job, which is the exact opposite of the point.

| **Executive, Tier 3** | **14** — scenario 3 or 5 | 7 Pilot, then 12 Attrition | 9 Speed · 2 Cost | Long narratives | Money, speed, a pilot that de-risks the call |
| **Nodal / grievance** | **14** — scenario 6, *as a question* | None — referral ask | 13 framed as a question | Any pitch | Their read on root causes, and a name |
| **Existing client** | **14** — scenario 8 or 11 | 6 — Workshops | 4 Measurement · 5 Their own case study | 11 Credibility | Expansion, refresh, an honest read on what worked |

The cold opener column is the first message. The lead angle is what carries the conversation
once they have replied. Scenario numbers are from `references/scenarios.md`.

**One angle per asset.** An asset that argues five things argues none.
