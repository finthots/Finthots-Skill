# The assets — what each one must contain

Output `.docx` for written assets, `.pptx` or a deck artifact for presentations, `.xlsx` for
models. Editable files by default.

---

## 1. Case study one-pager — build these first

**Structure.** Situation (2–3 sentences on the client's problem, anonymised where permission
is unclear) · What was built (format, hours, design level, languages, delivery mode) · What
changed (the honest outcome — adoption, mandate, scale, coverage; a measured figure only if
one genuinely exists) · A client quote where permission exists.

**One page. One client. One angle.**

Best three candidates:
- **Middle East bank retail assets programme** — 21-hour eLearning plus a 15-day workshop for
  the entire retail assets team, with impact assessment, *since made mandatory for every new
  joiner and followed by a 5-day level-up workshop.* That mandate is the outcome; it needs no
  invented percentage.
- **Gamified Property Visit module** for an NBFC's mortgage team — the most relevant single
  artefact for any housing finance prospect.
- **Vehicle finance eLearning, 32 hours across 6 businesses in 9 languages** — proves
  multilingual production at scale.

**Never** pad with a percentage that was not measured. "Made mandatory for every new joiner"
is stronger than a fabricated number and cannot be challenged.

## 2. Capability deck, cut to a segment

Ten to twelve slides. Lead with the segment's own wedge — mortgage underwriting and property
visit for housing finance; sourcing and asset quality for vehicle finance; field discipline
and collections conduct for microfinance.

Order: who FinThots is (one slide, RBI credential on it) · the problem in their segment ·
what gets built · design levels L1–L3 · languages and delivery · two case studies ·
measurement · the ask.

Cut the generic corporate-training slides entirely. A BFSI buyer is unimpressed by breadth
and persuaded by depth in their own vertical.

## 3. Workshop menu one-pager

A table: topic · duration · audience · mode (offline / online / blended). Group by vertical —
mortgage, SME, vehicle, collections, mandatory.

Needed for every past client and every credit head. Angle 6 cannot be used without it.

## 4. Demo reel script — two minutes

**The single highest-return asset to build**, because it is what interested replies ask for
and currently nothing exists to send.

Shoot from existing modules. Script beats: a module opening on a *phone* (not a desktop) ·
a regional-language voiceover · one interaction the viewer can see working · a knowledge
check · the completion dashboard. No narrator explaining features; let the screen do it.

Under two minutes. Hosted, not attached.

## 5. Skill assessment instrument — the strongest step on the ladder

Fifteen to twenty questions per role: mortgage credit underwriting, collections, compliance,
field sourcing. Scenario-based, not definitional — "the borrower presents X, what do you do"
rather than "what is LTV".

The prospect's team takes it; FinThots returns a **gap report** showing where capability is
thin by role and region.

This converts a sales conversation into a consulting engagement, surfaces their real gaps,
and produces the measured outcome data that unlocks Angle 1 for the next prospect. Nobody
else in this market offers it, and skill assessment is already a listed FinThots capability.

## 6. In-house vs FinThots cost model (.xlsx)

Blank, for them to fill with their own numbers: ID salary and months, SME time, review
cycles, studio or tooling cost, rework, opportunity cost of a six-month delay. Then the
FinThots line from the wallet grid.

Turns Angle 2 from a discount claim into their own arithmetic, which is far more persuasive
and cannot be argued with.

## 7. Impact assessment one-pager

What gets measured, how, at what points, and what the client receives. Angle 4 is the lead
angle for L&D heads and currently has no artefact behind it.

## 8. Attrition cost calculator (.xlsx)

Cost per leaver, time-to-productivity, and the effect of structured induction on both. For HR
heads and Tier 3 MDs — the only money-framed asset in the set.

# The CTA ladder

| Step | Stage | What they get | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cold contact | One angle plus one credibility line. The ask *is* the asset | Have |
| 2 | They reply | **2-minute demo reel** | Build — priority 1 |
| 3 | They want depth | Gated sample module link, time-limited | Build |
| 4 | "Does it work?" | **Case study one-pager** | Build — priority 1 |
| 5 | Evaluating | Free 30-minute **capability review** — a structured diagnostic, not a demo | Build a question set |
| 6 | Want proof for their own people | **Free skill assessment** → gap report | Build — priority 1 |
| 7 | Ready to scope | Proposal with wallet pricing | Have |

Step 2 is where most interested replies die for want of something to send. Step 6 is the
differentiator.

**Price enters at step 7. Never before.**

Call step 5 a capability review, not a sales call, and arrive with ten diagnostic questions
rather than a deck — otherwise it becomes a demo and the diagnostic value is lost.
