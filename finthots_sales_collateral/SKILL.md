# FinThots Sales Collateral

Builds the client-facing assets that support a FinThots pitch, and decides which angle to
lead with for a given buyer.

Sits between two other skills: `finthots_client_leads` finds the buyer and writes the
per-contact outreach message; this skill builds the reusable assets that outreach points at;
`storyline-storyboard` produces the course once it is sold. Find → pitch → deliver.

The assets here are **segment-agnostic**. A case study or workshop menu serves housing
finance, vehicle finance and microfinance equally. Do not rebuild them per campaign.

## The one rule that matters most

**Never invent an outcome number.** No conversion percentages, no "30% improvement", no
efficiency gains, unless the figure came from a real impact assessment and you can say how it
was measured, over what period, against what. A credit head will ask. One unsupported number
ends the deal and the relationship.

Where a number would help but none exists, use the future tense instead: *we measure what
changed* rather than *we improved it by X*. That is honest, it is already a real FinThots
capability, and delivering it is what produces the number for the next prospect.

## Step 1 — Pick the angle before writing anything

Thirteen angles, their claims, proof status and cautions are in `references/angles.md`.
The persona mapping is in the same file. In short:

| Buyer | Lead with | Never lead with |
|---|---|---|
| L&D head | **We measure the improvement** | Price |
| Credit / risk / business head | **We already know your problem** | Price, LMS convenience |
| Compliance officer | **Regulatory obligation, not preference** | Anything about improvement |
| HR head | **Attrition economics** | The credit diagnostic |
| Executive at a small lender | **Attrition economics**, then a pilot | Long capability narratives |
| Nodal / grievance officer | Nothing — this is a referral ask | Any pitch at all |
| Existing or past client | **Workshops**, and an honest review of earlier work | Credibility lines they already know |

Three angles are ready and underused: **nine Indian languages produced in-house** (a genuine
moat for rural and affordable lenders), **fits existing habits** (mobile, between field
visits, in their own language), and **the RBI credential** (a regulator accepted the
production standard, which is what makes any first message get read).

## Step 2 — Build the asset

Each asset has its own spec in `references/assets.md`: what it must contain, what it must
never contain, length, and which proof points belong in it. The assets are:

1. **Case study one-pager** — the highest-value thing to build, and the one most often missing
2. **Capability deck**, cut to a segment
3. **Workshop menu one-pager** — topic, duration, audience, mode
4. **Demo reel script** — two minutes, a module on a phone, in a regional language
5. **Skill assessment instrument** — a diagnostic their team takes; you return a gap report
6. **In-house vs FinThots cost model** — blank, they fill it with their own numbers
7. **Impact assessment one-pager** — what gets measured, how, what they receive
8. **Attrition cost calculator**

Output format: `.docx` for one-pagers and written assets, `.pptx` or a deck artifact for
presentations, `.xlsx` for models and calculators. Use the matching skill for each format.
Deliver editable files by default.

The full list with context is in `references/proof.md`. Use these, invent nothing. The ones
that carry the most weight:

  client-facing.

- The **Middle East bank retail assets programme** — 21-hour eLearning plus a 15-day workshop

  new joiner. The strongest possible endorsement and the best case study candidate.

- **32 hours of vehicle finance eLearning in 9 languages**; collections simulations in 9
  languages. The multilingual proof.

Scale: 200+ hours of eLearning, 100,000+ BFSI professionals trained, 45+ trainers and SMEs,
40+ clients across India, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka and Oman, in-house ID team and recording
studio, design levels L1–L3.

**Check the client list against the prospect before writing.** Several client groups own

## Step 4 — The CTA ladder

Full version in `references/assets.md`. The progression:

cold contact → 2-minute demo reel → sample module link → case study → **free 30-minute
capability review** (not a demo; a structured diagnostic conversation) → **free skill
assessment** → proposal with wallet pricing.

The skill assessment is the strongest step on the ladder. It converts a sales conversation
into a consulting engagement, surfaces their real gaps, and produces the outcome data that
makes the next pitch quantified rather than hopeful. Nobody else in this market offers it.

Price enters at the proposal, never before.

## Brand and tone

- **FinThots®** with the ® on first mention only; plain FinThots thereafter.
- FinThots is the sole client-facing brand. **Sunyukth Newwave LLP is reserved strictly for
  legal, financial and regulatory documents** and never appears in client-facing material.
- Client-facing tone: warm and substantial without overwhelming. Firm but diplomatic on IP
  and pricing. Conversational for networking contexts.
- Concise by default. Shorter is almost always better unless depth is explicitly requested.
- When a reference document is supplied as a format benchmark, follow it closely rather than
  inventing a new structure.
- Preserve existing template formatting, branding and structure when updating a file.

## Hard rules

1. Never invent an outcome number, percentage or client result.
2. Never name a client without checking the engagement is real and nameable; anonymise when
   permission is unclear ("a leading private sector bank").
3. Never lead with price. It anchors FinThots as the cheap option and buyers who arrive on
   price leave on price.
4. Never put Sunyukth Newwave LLP in client-facing material.
5. Pick one angle per asset. An asset that argues five things argues none.
6. Deliver editable files unless told otherwise.
7. Check whether the prospect's group is already a client before writing a cold line.
